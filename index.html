Design Pattern

This project was initiated by my supervisor to develop a design pattern for forms, aiming to unify our design approach across the company. The goal was to create a consistent pattern for forms based on the organization's current design language.

Duration: 5 weeks Role: Project owner and solo UX designer
Discover

Research of Design Patterns

The process began with researching the architecture of design patterns and identifying best practices for documentation. As this project aimed to set the foundations for future usage, a structure had to be established. I used design systems like Carbon and Polaris as inspiration for the structure.

Research of Existing Products

Next, I audited all existing products within the organization to analyse how components are currently being used. During this audit, I conducted a pattern inventory to map out which components are used, when they appear, and their specific behaviors.

Define

Design Decisions

The overview provided by the inventory made it much easier to make informed design decisions. By combining theoretical research with interaction design best practices, I determined which components to loop and which needed to be added.

Ideate

First Draft

Based on those decisions, I created an initial draft to visualise how the documentation could be structured.

Test

Workshop

In this draft, I facilitated a workshop with the UX team. 13 participants were tasked with building a new form using only the existing documentation, which allowed us to identify gaps and areas that needed further clarification.

The workshop helped me get an objective view of the existing version and made me realise that we needed to clarify scope by using examples, making more of an inspiration of why we are doing the way we are doing it, and overall making the documentation deeper.

Ideate & Prototyping

Iteration

The insights gathered from the workshop were used to revise the documentation, making it more comprehensive and robust. I added detailed guidance on decision making logic, clearer visual examples, and additional components that were missing.

Deliver

Future Plans

The documentation will now be published within our design system, making it easily accessible to all stakeholders. This will be a powerful resource for onboarding new team members by providing an immediate overview of our ecosystem, and acts as a clear reference point for non-designers to understand our standards.

Key impacts

Streamlined Onboarding: A smoother, more efficient introduction for new team members.
Enhanced Decision-Making: Faster and more effective design decisions backed by documented best practices.
Cross-Platform Alignment: Greater visual and functional consistency across all digital surfaces.
Strategic Workflow: Implementation of a new way of working centered around shared design patterns.
Scalable Foundation: A solid base established for future audits, inventories, and pattern development.

CRM system

This project was initiated by the CRM product owners to simplify specific functions and flows, aiming to ease the daily workload for the product specialists using the system. The focus was wholly on streamlining particular processes rather than redesigning the entire system.

Duration: 8 weeks (ongoing) Role: UX researcher in a team of two
Discover

Research CRM

The process started by researching CRM architectures and industry best practices. I conducted benchmarking of various systems using Maldot to gather inspiration and standards.

Evaluation of Product

I performed a heuristic analysis of the current CRM system. By combining it with benchmarking and UX best practices, I could identify what was currently working and where the friction points were.

Define

Developer Collaboration

We set up meetings with the developers to get a clearer picture of the technical expectations and project goals. To ensure everyone stayed aligned, we established bi-weekly syncs between the design and development teams.

Ideate

Wireframing

We developed wireframes to create a concrete visualisation of the problems and explored various design solutions through different proposals.

Test

User Testing

These wireframes were used in user tests with the product specialists who use the system daily. The tests began by observing their current workflow to understand pain points, then participants were asked to use the new flow to compare the experience.

Product Owner Alignment

Following the tests, we met with the product owners to share our insights. We discussed what was feasible to implement considering current organizational and technical constraints, prioritizing features based on the direct feedback from our users.

Ideate & Prototyping

Iteration

The wireframes then evolved from Lo-Fi to Mid-Fi and finally Hi-Fi. Throughout each cycle, we maintained constant alignment with the involved teams through scheduled meetings and syncs.

High-Fidelity Prototyping

A final prototype was built to create the most realistic product experience possible.

Test

Final User Testing

With the high-fidelity prototype ready, we conducted a second round of user testing with the specialists. This allowed us to refine the UX at a granular level and further simplify the interaction and overall flow.

Deliver

Future Plans

These designs were then handed over to the product owners and developers. It is now going into production for the upcoming launch of the new version.

Key impacts

Task Optimisation: Simplified critical user tasks, making complex functions more accessible and intuitive.
Operational Efficiency: Streamlined core workflows, significantly reducing the time and manual effort required per task.
Improved Onboarding: The redesigned interface is easier for new users to learn, creating a more straightforward and welcoming first experience.
User-Supported Features: Feature prioritisation was directly driven by real user feedback, ensuring the most impactful changes were implemented first.
Platform Consolidation: Reduced tool fragmentation by centralising major processes into a single, cohesive flow.

WCAG Documentation

This project was my thesis work, driven by the ambition to highlight the challenges of WCAG 2.x, specifically its technical language, heavy text density, and difficult navigation. The goal was to develop a generic proposal for structuring various elements to clarify information, rather than creating entirely new documentation.

Duration: 4 weeks Role: Solo UX researcher & designer
Discover

Research on WCAG, Design Principles & Best Practices

I began with extensive research into WCAG and its current documentation. I also explored best practices for designing documentation that handles heavy information-dense text, examining which principles focused on simplifying complex information.

Survey

To understand how UX designers interact with the documentation today, I distributed a survey to 20 UX professionals who work with WCAG. The goal was to map out their current pains and gains when navigating accessibility requirements.

Define

Thematic Analysis

Based on the survey results, I performed a thematic analysis which clearly showed a significant need to make accessibility requirements more accessible themselves. Many respondents noted that they often rely on "simplified" versions because the official documentation feels overwhelming and difficult to digest.

Heuristic Analysis

Following the thematic analysis, I conducted a heuristic analysis comparing a simplified version of the WCAG requirements against the original. I used a framework that systematically evaluates the pros and cons of different information hierarchies.

Test

User Testing

I designed a user test where participants were tasked with finding and understanding a specific accessibility requirement using both the simplified and original versions. They then evaluated which version was more intuitive and why. The test was conducted with 7 UX designers.

Ideate

Design Decisions

With all these insights gathered, I compiled a prioritized list of features. This included elements that users found essential and beneficial, as well as identifying the specific factors that hindered their workflow.

Prototyping

Prototyping

I started by creating wireframes in Figma to establish the layout. These were then combined with the features from the analysis to produce a prototype. This prototype demonstrated how a simplified presentation of accessibility requirements could look, using specific UI elements to make the experience more intuitive.

Deliver

Future Plans

Simplifying and increasing the accessibility of these requirements is something every organisation should prioritise. By making the documentation easier to use for those building digital products, we can more effectively create inclusive designs that benefit everyone and contribute to a more equitable society.

Key impacts

Documentation Framework: Established a new, scalable structure for internal documentation, setting a standard for how information is organised and shared.
Streamlined Accessibility Compliance: Optimised the workflow for accessibility testing, reducing time spent on repetitive and technical tasks.
Enhanced Inclusive Design: Directly improved the accessibility of the final products, ensuring a better experience for all users.
User-Centric Innovation: Successfully implemented highly requested features, directly addressing documented user pain points.
Increased Organisational Awareness: Cultivated a deeper understanding of accessibility standards and their importance across the organisation.
